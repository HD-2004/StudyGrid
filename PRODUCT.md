# Product

## Register

product

## Users

StudyGrid serves college and university students balancing multiple subjects,
exam deadlines, fixed commitments, and changing amounts of study time. They
use the product while planning a week, deciding what to study next, or
recovering after a missed or ineffective session. The primary job is to turn
course material and real availability into a plan that remains usable when
life or recall differs from the original schedule.

## Product Purpose

StudyGrid combines material analysis, deterministic scheduling, spaced review,
and progress feedback in one persistent workflow. Success means a student can
understand what to study, when to review it, why the plan changed, and what did
not fit without manually rebuilding a calendar. AI may help interpret material
or explain the plan, but it never silently controls calendar placement.

## Brand Personality

Calm, credible, and adaptive. StudyGrid should reduce planning anxiety, state
capacity limits honestly, and feel dependable enough to use during a busy
semester. Its voice is concise and supportive without sounding childish,
clinical, or overly enthusiastic.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards that decorate every metric with gradients, glass,
  oversized cards, or marketing-style claims.
- Loud gamification, streak pressure, confetti, and competitive mechanics that
  turn study difficulty into a moral judgment.
- Opaque AI interfaces that hide whether a result came from a model, imply
  false certainty, or change a schedule without an explanation.
- Rigid calendar tools that present over-capacity plans as if all work fits.

## Design Principles

1. Put the calendar and the student's next decision ahead of product chrome.
2. Explain every adaptation and distinguish deterministic scheduling from AI
   assistance.
3. Model real constraints honestly, including missed work, limited capacity,
   recovery time, and exam cutoffs.
4. Prefer familiar controls and progressive disclosure so students can edit
   detailed inputs without losing the main workflow.
5. Keep the complete planning flow useful when external AI services are
   unavailable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the user-test surface. All core workflows must be
keyboard operable, expose clear accessible names and status updates, retain
meaning without relying on color alone, respect reduced-motion preferences,
and remain usable at phone widths and browser zoom. Copy should avoid shame or
pressure when a student misses work or recalls material poorly.
